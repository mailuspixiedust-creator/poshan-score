How we rate

Every ceiling on this page is a real, cited number — never a rounded-off guess. Nothing here is a comparative claim against a named brand; it's a standard, applied evenly to everyone, including us.

Our sourcing order, in full: for every metric we start with India's own NIN/FSSAI standards. If NIN has no usable number for that metric or age band, we fall through in this fixed order until one has real data: NIN → WHO → US NASEM → EFSA (EU) → UK SACN. Whichever source in that chain first has an actual figure is the one we cite — we don't skip ahead just because a later source is more convenient. Each card below shows exactly which sources were checked and which one we landed on.

Added sugar

Free sugars as a share of the product's energy, checked against the age band's ceiling.

Used: WHO — <10% of energy (target <5%)
✗ NIN/FSSAI — qualitative "use sparingly" only, no number
✓ WHO 2015 — used (converted to grams via NIN energy RDA per age band)

Sodium

Milligrams per day against the age band's ceiling.

Used: US NASEM — 800 / 1,000 / 1,200 / 1,500 mg
✗ NIN — adult-only, and two NIN documents disagree (6g salt vs 2,000mg sodium)
✗ WHO 2012 — states the scaling principle only, gives no child figure
✓ NASEM 2019 — used (age-banded mg figures)
cross-checked against EFSA 2019 (1,100/1,300/1,700/2,000mg) and SACN 2003 (800/1,200/2,000/2,400mg) — broadly convergent

Artificial additives

Synthetic colours, flavours and emulsifiers not needed for shelf stability.

Used: FSSAI Food Additives Regulations, 2011
✓ NIN/FSSAI — used directly (permitted-additive list by INS code and category; this is India's applicable law, not a nutrient-intake ceiling, so the intake waterfall doesn't apply here)

Preservatives

Chemical preservatives beyond what shelf-stable co-manufacturing requires.

Used: FSSAI Food Additives Regulations, 2011
✓ NIN/FSSAI — used directly (permitted preservative classes and INS codes, e.g. benzoates, sulphites, antioxidants like BHA/TBHQ, by food category)

Hydrogenated fats

Industrial trans fat content, flagged against the legal ceiling.

Used: FSSAI — ≤2% by mass of total oils/fats
✓ NIN/FSSAI — used directly (hard regulatory ceiling, effective 1 Jan 2022)
cross-checked against EU's ~2g/100g fat limit (2019) and WHO's global elimination target — consistent direction

Base-ingredient integrity

Whether the first three ingredients are recognisable whole foods, not refined derivatives.

No external ceiling — our own internal rubric
No government body sets a numeric "whole-food-ness" standard. This check is ours, disclosed as such — made possible by FSSAI's mandatory rule that ingredients be listed in descending order by weight.
Every ceiling above is reviewed as source documents are updated. If a source is later superseded (e.g. we obtain the primary ICMR-NIN 2020 EAR report directly), the card and this page are updated together — never silently.

Sodium — following the sourcing waterfall

Per our stated order (NIN → WHO → US NASEM → EFSA → SACN — see How We Rate), NIN gives adult-only figures that disagree across its own two documents, and WHO 2012 states only the scaling principle with no child number. NASEM 2019 is the first source in the chain with real age-banded figures, so it's what we use:

Age bandNASEM 2019 sodium AI
0–6 moBreastmilk adequate
1–3 yr800 mg/day
4–6 yr1,000 mg/day (NASEM band: 4–8yr)
7–9 yr
10–12 yr1,200 mg/day (NASEM band: 9–13yr)

Source: NASEM (2019), Dietary Reference Intakes for Sodium and Potassium. Note the band mismatch — NASEM's bands (1–3 / 4–8 / 9–13 / 14–18yr) don't line up cleanly with ours, so 4–6yr and 7–9yr share one NASEM figure. Cross-checked against the next two in the waterfall — EFSA 2019 (1,100/1,300/1,700/2,000mg, which does align to our bands) and UK SACN 2003 (800/1,200/2,000/2,400mg, explicit population reduction targets rather than AI). All three are broadly convergent; German DGE 2018 runs notably lower (400/500/750/1,100mg) due to different methodology and isn't part of our stated chain.

About

Poshan Score exists to make one thing easy: knowing what's actually in the food a child is handed, before it goes in the tiffin box.

We score against public standards — ICMR-NIN 2020 and FSSAI — not against other brands. The name nods to India's Poshan Abhiyaan, and the standard is applied the same way to every product on this site, including our own.
