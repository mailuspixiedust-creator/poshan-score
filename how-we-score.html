How the Poshan Score works

Every food on this site runs through the same rubric. Nothing is scored by hand, and no brand can pay to move a number. This page is the whole method, generated from the same rules the site runs on — so it cannot quietly drift out of date.

Two measuring sticks

A packet prints its numbers per 100 g. A cooked meal is judged as the plate a child actually eats. Scoring both the same way would punish home food for being mostly water, so each gets its own thresholds. The bands at the end are identical.

What removes points

Sugar here means added sugar, not the sugar already in fruit or milk. That distinction is why a fruit puree and a fruit drink end up in very different places.

What the bands mean

What the score does not tell you

It reads the label, not your child. It cannot see an allergy, a doctor's instruction, or the fact that your seven-year-old will only eat three things this month. It says nothing about taste, cost per serving, or how a food fits the rest of the day. Treat it as a shortcut through the ingredient list, not a verdict.

Thresholds are set against ICMR-NIN 2020 recommended intakes for Indian children, FSSAI labelling rules and WHO guidance on free sugars. When those change, this page and every score on the site change with them.
